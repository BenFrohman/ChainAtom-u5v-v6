# What 31 and 35 are

Author: Benjamin Stanley Frohman. © 2026. Apache-2.0.

They were produced. They are not μ.

A computer that walks monomials against a *partial* leading-term list will print a finite number if you also cap the box. That number is the size of the box complement. It is a real output of that walk. It is the wrong invariant.

## 35

Leading terms used: `{u^4, u v^5}` — **v^{11} omitted**.
Cutoff: `j ≤ 19` (twenty powers of v).
Count: `20 + 3·5 = 35`.
The ray `{v^j}` never dies. Bigger cutoff → bigger print. Unbounded without v^{11}.

## 31

Leading terms used: `{u^5, u v^5}` — **u^4 and v^{11} omitted**.
Cutoff: `j ≤ 10` (eleven powers of v).
Count: `11 + 4·5 = 31`.
Same ray, different cap, extra u^4-row still alive.

## 26

Leading terms used, all three, none skipped: `{u^4, u v^5, v^{11}}`.
Count: `11 + 3·5 = 26`.
Independent of cutoff once those three LTs are present.
Matches weights and I = 6+20.

So 31 and 35 are honest answers to a different question: *how many monomials in this box avoid this incomplete list?* The Milnor number is the dimension after the *ideal* is closed. Closing the ideal puts v^{11} in it.
